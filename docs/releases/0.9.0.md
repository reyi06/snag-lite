# Snag Lite 0.9.0

Released 2026-10-28.

Snag Lite is the lightweight side-by-side fork of Snag. It keeps the public-link workflow, shared queue, history, Scroll mode, and adaptive 1080p/1440p/2160p downloads, while removing account sign-in and the large FFmpeg Android package.

Snag Lite installs beside the original Snag. Android 10 or newer and a 64-bit ARM device are required.

## Highlights

- **About 23 MB instead of about 100 MB.** R8 full-mode shrinking and resource shrinking bring the DEX/resource footprint down, and the full FFmpeg Android AAR is gone.
- **Small static FFmpeg 7.1.** A minimal static binary handles stream-copy merging for H.264, HEVC, VP9, AV1, AAC, and Opus. Unlike the old package, it does not unpack a 34 MB FFmpeg runtime.
- **One yt-dlp copy.** yt-dlp is stored once as a raw resource and installed into the private runtime. The duplicate asset was removed.
- **No account system.** There is no TikTok login, Instagram login, cookie export, session validation, or account section in Settings.
- **Side-by-side package.** The Lite app uses `com.snag.downloader.lite` and the label `Snag Lite`.

## Verification

- YouTube 4K download produced a valid `3840×2160` VP9 video with AAC audio.
- A 10-second 4K AV1 + Opus source remuxed to a valid MP4 with the static FFmpeg binary.
- YouTube 144p VP9 + AAC smallest-file merge produced a valid MP4.
- TikTok link resolution and saving completed on the local emulator.
- Settings opens without the account section and no sign-in screen is reachable.
- Both arm64 and x86_64 release builds compile with R8 enabled; the published APK is arm64 only.

## Known limitations

- Instagram posts that require a logged-in session, including some public reels, cannot be downloaded. The app reports that the post needs an Instagram login and that Lite does not support sign-in.
- Private, removed, age-gated, or otherwise restricted posts remain unsupported.
- YouTube playlist resolution is still capped at the first 10 entries.
- The first YouTube or Instagram request extracts the Python runtime and can take longer than later requests.
